\begin{abstract}
We tested one minimal architecture change intended to close the paper-relevant
chemical-motif gap between the current neural MS2LDA model and Tomotopy. The
experiment kept seed 42, 1,000 topics, six CPU threads, frozen training and
validation data, existing token routing and losses, and the fixed epoch-40
checkpoint. It multiplied aggregated token topic mass by the square root of
existing whole-spectrum topic evidence before normalization. On leakage-filtered
validation data, useful high-confidence motifs---topics associated at probability
at least 0.5 with compound-balanced structure overlap score (SOS) at least
0.6---increased from 20 to 83, while Tomotopy produced 138. The candidate
therefore closed 53.4 percent of the gap and passed the predeclared SOS,
likelihood, and stability checks. However, MAG annotation coverage reached only
42.0 percent, below the required 49.6 percent. The candidate was rejected and
its test split was not accessed. The result supports document-level gating as a
useful direction, but not this architecture as the new paper model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and frozen comparison}

MS2LDA treats spectra as documents, fragments and neutral losses as words, and
recurring word distributions as Mass2Motifs \cite{vanderhooft2016}. The current
neural checkpoint has better likelihood and much faster direct inference than
the established Tomotopy model, but fewer chemically annotated and useful
high-confidence motifs. This experiment asks whether one document-level gate can
close that motif gap without a new encoder, loss, or parameter sweep.

The comparison fixes seed 42, 1,000 requested topics, six CPU threads, the
training/validation split, vocabulary, token features, deterministic
initialization, optimization schedule, and final epoch 40. The development
runner exposes no candidate test matrices or test records. MAG uses an existing
hash-verified index that excludes exactly the union of validation and test
compound connectivity keys.

\section{Document-gated mixture}

The current router produces sparse top-2 token assignments and existing
whole-spectrum document logits. After count-weighted token aggregation, the
candidate calculates
\[
g_d=\operatorname{softmax}(\text{document logits}/T),\qquad
\theta_d=\operatorname{normalize}(\text{token mass}\times g_d^{0.5}).
\]
The same calculation is used in training and inference. Multiplication cannot
create topic support absent from token routing, and an empty spectrum retains
the existing uniform fallback. Setting the new mixture weight to zero exactly
recovers the previous calculation. The gate adds no learned parameter, encoder,
or auxiliary loss.

\section{Paper-aligned validation}

MAG annotation and SOS use validation spectra only. Library entries sharing a
validation or test connectivity key are excluded before retrieval. Topic
association follows the paper threshold, $\theta_{dk}\geq0.5$. SOS is averaged
over unique compounds within each topic. Eligible topics are divided into high
($>0.8$), intermediate ($0.6$--$0.8$, inclusive), and low ($<0.6$) bands.

The primary outcome is the number of high-confidence motifs with SOS at least
0.6. Acceptance required all of the following:
\begin{itemize}
  \item closing at least 50 percent of the current-neural--Tomotopy useful-motif gap;
  \item MAG annotation coverage of at least 49.6 percent;
  \item mean high-confidence SOS no more than 0.02 below the current neural model;
  \item validation NLL no greater than 8.5266; and
  \item finite, stable training and evaluation.
\end{itemize}

The original experiment plan also proposed a 5,571-second fitting-time gate.
After observing that the run was slow, the investigator explicitly demoted this
condition to descriptive context. This post-run rule change is reported for
transparency. It does not affect the scientific decision because the unchanged
annotation-coverage gate rejects the candidate independently.

The document gate increases high-confidence associated validation spectra from
87 to 582 and SOS-evaluable high-confidence motifs from 42 to 156. Tomotopy has
446 associated spectra and 206 evaluable motifs. Of those evaluable motifs, 20,
83, and 138 respectively have SOS at least 0.6. Thus the candidate recovers 63
of the 118 motifs in the original gap, or 53.4 percent, exceeding the 50 percent
primary gate.

Quality among high-confidence motifs does not deteriorate. Candidate mean SOS
is 0.6373 versus 0.6227 for the current neural model, and median SOS rises from
0.5866 to 0.6185. Tomotopy remains higher at mean 0.6761 and median 0.6854.
Candidate validation NLL is 8.4166, 0.68 percent above the current 8.3594 but
inside the 8.5266 safety bound. Training and validation remain finite and stable.

The failure is breadth of annotation. MAG optimizes 420 candidate motifs,
compared with 384 for the current neural model and 607 for Tomotopy. Candidate
coverage is therefore 42.0 percent: an improvement of 3.6 percentage points,
but 7.6 points short of the predeclared 49.6 percent threshold. The architecture
is rejected, the checkpoint hash is retained as evidence, and candidate test
evaluation is not authorized.

Model fitting takes 177.8 minutes for the candidate, versus 84.4 minutes for the
current neural run and 76.0 minutes for the reused Tomotopy source run. This is
reported descriptively after the investigator removed runtime from acceptance;
the candidate would have failed the original runtime rule as well.

the model and batch are already loaded and one warm-up has completed. It is a

preprocessing, and first-call setup and is not end-to-end application latency.

\section{Interpretation}

The experiment confirms the frozen-checkpoint diagnosis: document evidence can
convert diffuse mixtures into many more confident, chemically useful motifs.
The improvement survives retraining and closes slightly more than half of the
target gap. However, it does not make enough of the full 1,000-topic inventory
annotatable, and it substantially increases fitting time. The likely next step
is not a weight sweep on this rejected candidate. A new architecture should
target broad motif annotatability directly while preserving the successful
confidence mechanism and the current likelihood baseline.

This remains a single-seed research experiment. The fixed seed is deliberate:
architecture development precedes multi-seed confirmation. No claim is made
that the candidate replaces the production Tomotopy backend.
