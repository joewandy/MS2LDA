\begin{abstract}
Classical mass-spectrometry latent Dirichlet allocation (MS2LDA) discovers
reusable Mass2Motifs from co-occurring fragments and
neutral losses, but its iterative inference limits amortized deployment. We
evaluated whether discovery itself can be made fully neural without a
Tomotopy teacher, DreaMS embeddings, variational Bayes (VB), or chemistry
labels and test information in the frozen run's parameter training. A direct
spectrum-level neural topic model collapsed;
sparse token routing with balanced Sinkhorn assignments and deterministic
topic recycling was associated with recovery from gross usage collapse, while
an embedding-regularized neural topic model (ERNTM) constraint reduced
semantic duplication. The historical epoch-36 checkpoint
replayed exactly. A fresh fixed-protocol run, performed without retuning,
selected epoch 38 and obtained held-out negative log-likelihood (NLL) 8.3563,
top-word diversity 0.7354, mean normalized pointwise mutual information (NPMI)
$-0.5699$, 132 corpus-active topics, and 231.5 mass-99 distinct-topic
equivalents. Its cached one-pass inference reached 5,529.0 spectra/s. Tomotopy
K=1000 remained stronger on NPMI ($-0.2996$), diversity (0.8145), active topics
(363), and chemical coverage. The result is therefore
a working, fast, collapse-resistant fully neural research model, although it
does not yet achieve Tomotopy parity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
MS2LDA transfers the document--topic--word abstraction to tandem mass
spectrometry (MS/MS): spectra are documents, fragments and neutral losses are words,
and recurring co-occurrence patterns are Mass2Motifs. These motifs can reflect
shared substructures and support unsupervised exploration beyond direct
library matching \cite{vanderhooft2016,ms2lda2026}. The classical approach is
well established, but both training and held-out inference are iterative.

Neural topic models promise fast amortized inference, flexible conditioning,
and differentiable integration with downstream models. They also have a known
failure mode: several nominal topics can converge to repetitive semantic
regions or receive negligible corpus mass \cite{wu2023}. Broader evaluations
have found that neural topic models can be less stable and less aligned with
applied categories than classical LDA \cite{hoyle2022}. This is especially
relevant for MS/MS, where documents are short and sparse, frequent peaks
dominate reconstruction gradients, rare motifs can still be chemically
valuable, and a large requested topic count $K$ creates many weakly supervised
components.

This work asks a bounded applied question: can published neural topic-model
regularization, combined with MS/MS-specific sparse routing, yield a useful
fully neural discovery model? It does not seek a new general machine-learning
algorithm. The contribution is a self-contained implementation, a
leakage-controlled benchmark, an exact replayable model bundle, and a candid
comparison with Tomotopy.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Data source and spectrum representation}
We used the public positive-ion-mode MSnLib assets in Zenodo record 20179680
(\url{https://zenodo.org/records/20179680}). The acquisition script downloads
only the two required archives, resumes interrupted transfers, rejects unsafe
archive entries, and verifies archive size, MD5 and SHA-256 digests, ZIP
integrity, and the hashes of extracted inputs. The primary input is
\texttt{Corinna\_Library\_filtered\_positive.mgf} (SHA-256
\texttt{c1a2389...714682f}), where MGF means Mascot Generic Format and each
block represents one tandem mass spectrum. A valid block requires a Universal
Spectrum Identifier (USI), feature identifier, Simplified Molecular Input
Line Entry System (SMILES) string, International Chemical Identifier key
(InChIKey), and precursor mass-to-charge ratio ($m/z$).

Preprocessing validates finite positive peak $m/z$ and non-negative intensity,
normalizes each original spectrum to maximum intensity one, retains peaks with
$0<m/z\leq2000$ and relative intensity 0.01--1.0, keeps at most the 1,000
strongest peaks, and rejects spectra with fewer than five retained peaks.
This retained 38,888 of 41,568 input blocks; 2,680 failed the declared parsing,
metadata, structure, or peak filters. A retained peak at $m/z=123.45$ becomes
the word \texttt{frag@123.45}. When precursor minus fragment mass exceeds
0.01, the same peak also yields a neutral-loss word such as
\texttt{loss@18.01}. Masses are rounded to two decimal places. Relative
intensity is represented as an integer count: both words are repeated
$\operatorname{round}(100\times\text{relative intensity})$ times. In the
sparse matrix this is stored as one nonzero vocabulary entry plus its count,
not as materialized duplicate tokens. A fragment and its derived loss form one
\emph{physical peak group}, which is always masked atomically to avoid leaking
the same measured peak across input and prediction target.

\subsection{Structural split, vocabulary, and held-out completion}
The RDKit cheminformatics toolkit parses each SMILES string and checks that its
derived connectivity block agrees with the supplied InChIKey. Cyclic molecules
are grouped by chirality-free Bemis--Murcko scaffold (their ring-and-linker
core); acyclic molecules are grouped by connectivity identity. Groups are
ordered by decreasing size, with a seed-42 hash tie-break, and greedily placed
to minimize normalized overflow relative to 70/10/20 percent targets. All
spectra from a group remain together. The resulting split contains 27,222
training, 3,889 validation, and 7,777 test spectra. An audit found zero overlap
of both compound connectivity keys and structural split groups.

Structural metadata is therefore used to create and audit the split, but no
SMILES, InChIKey, scaffold, chemical label, or library annotation is supplied
to the neural model or its objective. The 21,233-word vocabulary is then built
only from training spectra: words must occur in at least three training
documents, no corpus-frequency or top-frequency filter is applied, and column
order follows first appearance in the raw training spectra. A clean rebuild
reproduced this order exactly.

Validation and test use deterministic document completion. A seed-42 hash of
the spectrum identity and original peak index divides whole peak groups into
50 percent observed and 50 percent completion partitions, retaining at least
one group on each side. The observed half is renormalized using only its own
maximum, so even the held-out maximum cannot leak into inference. The fresh
completion target retains its full-spectrum count weights. The fresh test NLL
denominator contains 2,534,703 in-vocabulary completion-token copies
from 7,776 of 7,777 spectra. A further 70,016 copies (2.688 percent) are out of
vocabulary (OOV); they are reported but excluded from NLL.

\subsection{Neural architecture}
The model is a neural token-assignment topic model, rather than a conventional
variational autoencoder (VAE). It retains the MS2LDA admixture interpretation---each
spectrum is a mixture of topics and each topic is a distribution over fragment
and neutral-loss words---but replaces per-spectrum iterative inference with one
learned routing pass. There is no Gaussian latent variable,
Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence-to-prior term,
Tomotopy teacher, or local VB refinement.

For vocabulary word $w$, the fixed input feature $f_w\in\mathrm{R}^{64}$
concatenates a 48-dimensional skip-gram-with-negative-sampling (SGNS)
embedding learned only from training co-occurrences, 14 sine/cosine Fourier
mass features at seven frequencies, and a
two-dimensional fragment/loss indicator. A learned bias-free linear projection
maps this vector to a normalized 128-dimensional token embedding $e_w$.
Prototype initialization is also data-only: weighted k-means++ chooses 500
projected training-vocabulary tokens, using square-root corpus frequency times
squared inverse document frequency as the sampling weight, but performs no
Tomotopy initialization or Lloyd updates.

Routing is contextual at the level of an individual observed token. For token
$i$ in spectrum $d$, the model first forms the weighted mean $c_{di}$ of all
other token embeddings in that spectrum. A two-layer 256-unit multilayer
perceptron (MLP) predicts a correction to the token embedding,
\[
r_{di}=\operatorname{norm}\!\left[e_{w_i}+
g\!\left(e_{w_i},c_{di}\right)\right],
\qquad
\ell_{dik}=r_{di}^{\mathsf T}\bar t_k,
\]
where $t_k$ is a learned 128-dimensional topic prototype. Softmax logits use a
temperature annealed from 0.5 to 0.1. Only the two largest probabilities are
retained and renormalized; a straight-through estimator supplies gradients
through this discrete top-2 choice during training. The spectrum mixture is
then the normalized, token-weighted sum of those sparse assignments,
\[
\theta_{dk}=
\frac{\sum_{i\in d}x_{di}\,q_{dik}}
     {\sum_{i\in d}x_{di}},
\]
so the spectrum--topic mixture $\theta$ for a complete spectrum requires one router evaluation rather than an
optimization loop.

The decoder shares the same geometry instead of learning an unrelated dense
matrix. The topic--word matrix $\beta$ and the MS2LDA admixture likelihood are
\[
\beta_{kw}=\operatorname{softmax}_{w}
\left(2\,\bar{t}_k^{\mathsf T}\bar{e}_w/0.2\right),
\qquad
p(w\mid d)=\sum_k\theta_{dk}\beta_{kw}.
\]
Consequently each learned prototype has two linked roles: it attracts routed
tokens to produce $\theta$, and its similarity to every vocabulary embedding
defines the corresponding Mass2Motif row of $\beta$. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}
separates this one-pass inference path from the training-only controls.

\subsection{Training-only feature learning and initialization}
SGNS is trained before the topic model, using only training documents. It runs
for five epochs, draws eight positive co-occurrence pairs per document per
epoch in proportion to the intensity-derived counts, and contrasts each with
five negative words sampled according to corpus frequency raised to 0.75.
SparseAdam uses batch size 8,192 and learning rate 0.01. Averaged, normalized
source/context vectors form the 48 SGNS coordinates. These are concatenated
with 14 Fourier mass coordinates (sine and cosine at frequencies
1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 after scaling mass by 1,000) and a two-value
fragment/loss indicator, then normalized to yield the fixed 64-dimensional
feature.

The learned $64\rightarrow128$ projection is initialized orthogonally. Topic
prototypes are seeded by weighted k-means++ sampling without subsequent Lloyd
refinement; the weight is square-root corpus frequency times squared inverse
document frequency (IDF). Thus initialization uses spectral co-occurrence,
mass, type, and training frequency only. It does not copy topics from
Tomotopy or the published MSnLib motif set.

\subsection{Anti-collapse mechanism and optimization}
\subsubsection{Two failure modes}
Collapse has two distinct forms here. In \emph{usage collapse}, most observed
mass is routed through a small subset of prototypes and the others receive
almost no learning signal. In \emph{semantic collapse}, several active
prototypes occupy the same region and yield near-duplicate top words. The
training objective addresses these failure modes separately rather than
treating reconstruction loss as sufficient.

\subsubsection{Corrupted views and router objective}
Each epoch uses one of four deterministic pairs of corrupted training views;
each side independently retains 80\% of physical peak groups, with a fragment
and its derived loss kept together, and the pair rotates by epoch. Because the
views can overlap, this is not strict complementary masking; it reduces
identity shortcuts and trains robustness to missing peaks. Each retained view
is renormalized to its own maximum. In the router block,
the decoder probabilities $\beta$ are cached and detached once per epoch and
the mixture from either view must predict the tokens in the other:
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{router}}=
\tfrac12\!\left[\operatorname{NLL}(B\mid\theta^A,\beta)+
                 \operatorname{NLL}(A\mid\theta^B,\beta)\right]
+\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Sinkhorn}}
+0.1\,\operatorname{JS}(\theta^A,\theta^B),
\]
where JS is Jensen--Shannon divergence. Shared token projections and prototypes
can still change because they define routing, so ``cached decoder'' does not
mean that all decoder geometry is immutable for the epoch. Cross-view
completion discourages reliance only on a token's own identity, while JS
consistency asks the two partial views to describe the same spectrum similarly.
The routed token itself is excluded when its contextual mean is formed.

\subsubsection{Balanced Sinkhorn usage target}
The first anti-collapse control is a stop-gradient balanced Sinkhorn target.
Five log-space normalization iterations convert the token--prototype logits in
each mini-batch into a transport plan whose rows sum to one and whose aggregate
columns are equal. Cross-entropy trains the ordinary router probabilities
toward this plan. This is an early training scaffold, not a claim that chemical
motifs are uniformly prevalent: its weight is held at 0.25 for the first ten
epochs and then reduced linearly toward 0.05 at epoch index 40. The executed
run used zero-based indices 0--39, so its 40th and final epoch used weight
0.0567. This documents the frozen execution rather than silently changing it.
The schedule gives initially weak
prototypes enough routed examples to start learning while leaving
reconstruction increasingly responsible for final prevalence.

\subsubsection{ERNTM prototype separation}
The second control is the ERNTM prototype-separation constraint
\cite{shao2022}. For normalized prototypes, $S_{kj}=\bar t_k^{\mathsf T}\bar
t_j$, and the model minimizes row-wise cross-entropy with the identity matrix
as the target. Each prototype must therefore be more similar to itself than to
any competing prototype. This directly penalizes semantic duplication but
does not by itself guarantee that a topic is used. It is applied with weight
1.0 during four topic/decoder updates per epoch. Those updates hold the routed
assignments fixed and optimize cross-view completion plus a weight-0.25 local
decoder term that asks the selected topic rows of $\beta$ to emit their
assigned tokens.

\subsubsection{Deterministic topic recycling}
The third control is deterministic topic recycling. At validation every two
epochs, a topic is marked underused when its corpus assignment mass is less
than $0.1/K$. If this persists for three consecutive validations, its prototype
is replaced by a normalized routing context drawn from a bounded queue of the
highest reconstruction-loss tokens. The corresponding AdamW moment rows are
reset so stale optimizer state cannot pull the replacement back. A topic can
be recycled at most twice. Recycling is designed to restore learning
opportunity for a dead component; Sinkhorn supplies early usage pressure; and
ERNTM encourages surviving components to remain apart. Their combination
coincided with avoidance of gross collapse, but the frozen evidence does not
isolate each mechanism in a factorial ablation. None is treated as evidence of
chemical validity, which is measured only after training.

\subsubsection{Alternating optimization and checkpoint selection}
Optimization alternates one complete shuffled pass through all training
spectra for the router (batch size 128) with only four topic/decoder mini-batch
updates (batch size 512), not four further full passes. Router updates use
cached $\beta$; topic updates detach assignments and update the shared
topic/decoder geometry. AdamW, an adaptive optimizer with decoupled weight
decay, uses learning rate 0.001,
weight decay $10^{-4}$, and gradient-norm clipping at 5.0. Four central
processing unit (CPU) threads are
pinned. Training ends at 40 epochs and evaluates validation every two epochs.
Validation has two declared roles: it selects the checkpoint and supplies
corpus-usage estimates for recycling. A validation checkpoint is saved before
any recycling decision made at that validation. Selection maximizes validation
mass-99 distinct-topic equivalents, breaking ties with lower completion NLL
and then the earlier epoch. Test spectra and all chemistry are opened only
after the within-run checkpoint choice is frozen.

\subsection{Comparator and evaluation}
Tomotopy is the optimized software implementation of classical LDA used by
MS2LDA. The comparator uses $K=1000$, $\alpha=0.6$, $\eta=0.1$, seed 42, and
the same prepared training documents and 21,233-word vocabulary. It trains in
blocks of 50 iterations with six workers (parallel setting 3), stopping at
5,000 iterations or after the relative perplexity change remains below 0.005
for ten checks. Held-out inference uses 100 iterations and one worker.
Tomotopy is never an initializer or teacher.

This is an established-setting comparison, not a capacity- or core-matched
algorithm comparison: the neural model uses 500 topics and four CPU threads,
whereas Tomotopy uses 1,000 topics and one
worker for held-out inference. Both see the same spectra, completion masks,
vocabulary, and scoring functions, so completion and coherence metrics remain
informative. Counts whose threshold depends on $K$ are method-relative.

\subsection{Chemical annotation and evaluation}
Chemistry is evaluated only after model and checkpoint selection, using
$\theta$ inferred from each \emph{full} test spectrum. Automated Mass2Motif
Annotation Guidance (MAG) turns each topic's top 20 $\beta$ words and weights
into a pseudo-spectrum. Spec2Vec, a learned spectral-similarity embedding,
embeds that pseudo-spectrum; a FAISS vector-search index returns up to 1,000
candidate neighbors, from which ten unique full-InChIKey molecules are kept.
Before index construction, every validation and test connectivity key is
removed from the reference library and the retained index is audited for zero
leaks. Hits are clustered at cosine similarity 0.9 and passed through motif
optimization. A successful annotation is summarized by a MACCS structural-key
fingerprint whose bits occur in at least 80 percent of clustered structures.

Annotation coverage is the proportion of all requested topics for which MAG
optimization retains at least one fragment or loss. A topic becomes
SOS-evaluable only if it also has at least one associated held-out compound
with a valid fingerprint. Dominant association assigns each of the 7,777 test
spectra to its largest-$\theta$ topic. High-confidence association retains each
topic--spectrum pair with $\theta_k\geq0.5$; the reported count is therefore a
pair count (an exact 0.5 tie could associate one spectrum twice), not a
guaranteed count of unique spectra.

The structure overlap score (SOS) is asymmetric annotation containment,
\[
\operatorname{SOS}(a,m)=
\frac{|\operatorname{bits}(a)\cap\operatorname{bits}(m)|}
{|\operatorname{bits}(a)|},
\]
where $a$ is the consensus annotation and $m$ a held-out molecule. It ranges
from zero to one; higher means that more annotation bits occur in the molecule.
Within a topic, spectra are deduplicated by compound connectivity and compounds
are averaged equally; the reported global mean then averages eligible topics
equally. It is not spectrum-weighted. Coverage and conditional SOS answer
different questions and must be reported together.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Capacity and model selection}
Nominal $K$ was not treated as the number of usable motifs. Across the capacity
screen, increasing K expanded the mass-carrying inventory but usually reduced
global diversity. Embedding clustering regularization (ECR), adapted from the
published ECR topic model (ECRTM) \cite{wu2023}, improved selected diversity
points but did not dominate the joint likelihood--inventory frontier. The
architecture and $K=500$ choice were exploratory and informed by the earlier
study, which included test and chemistry comparisons. Once that family and
protocol were frozen, the fresh run selected epoch 38 using validation metrics
only; its test results are an end-to-end reproducibility checkpoint, not a new
untouched confirmatory study (Figure~\ref{fig:capacity}).

\subsection{Final non-chemical comparison}
The historical epoch-36 checkpoint replayed exactly under the cleaned code:
the derived $\beta$ was bit-for-bit identical, and all headline metrics were
reproduced. The fresh unattended 40-epoch run then selected epoch 38 by the
frozen validation rule. It reached test NLL 8.3563, diversity 0.7354, NPMI
$-0.5699$, 132 active topics, and 231.5 mass-99 distinct-topic equivalents.
These values are close to the replayed checkpoint (8.3694, 0.7592, $-0.5359$,
140, and 238.7 respectively) and preserve every central conclusion without
retuning. The neural model improved completion NLL relative to Tomotopy and
was 387-fold faster in the declared cached benchmark. This is a practical
pipeline result, not a same-core algorithmic ratio: neural routing used four
CPU threads, while Tomotopy used one worker and 100 inference iterations.
Tomotopy retained a larger, more coherent active inventory, although its
active threshold is lower because it uses twice as many requested topics
(Table~\ref{tab:comparison};
Figures~\ref{fig:comparison} and \ref{fig:speed}).

\subsection{Chemical evaluation}
MAG produced optimized annotations for 136/500 neural topics (27.2 percent),
versus 607/1000 Tomotopy topics (60.7 percent). Under dominant association,
105/500 neural topics (21.0 percent) were SOS-evaluable with conditional mean
SOS 0.6129, compared with 598/1000 (59.8 percent) at 0.6238. At probability at
least 0.5, 26/500 neural topics (5.2 percent) and 106 topic--spectrum
associations were evaluable, compared with 333/1000 (33.3 percent) and 961
associations for Tomotopy. The similar dominant conditional means show that
evaluable neural annotations have comparable annotation containment under
this procedure; without a shuffled or null baseline they do not establish
absolute chemical validity. Coverage remains the central weakness
(Figure~\ref{fig:chemistry}).

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Why fully neural MS2LDA is difficult}
The probabilistic and neural models share the same admixture likelihood; the
difference is how learning credit is assigned. Classical LDA repeatedly forms
token-to-topic expected counts, so a rare but consistent pattern can continue
to accumulate evidence. A gradient model can reduce reconstruction loss early
with a few broad components. Those components then receive more gradient,
while unused prototypes receive little signal and either die or become
duplicates. Avoiding a constant encoder (posterior collapse) is therefore not
enough: the global topic inventory can still undergo component collapse.

MS/MS intensifies this feedback because spectra are short relative to a
21,233-token vocabulary, common fragments and losses dominate token mass,
chemically valuable motifs can be rare, and useful per-spectrum mixtures must
be sparse even though the corpus-level inventory should be broad. Uniform use
is not chemically justified, yet unconstrained use collapses. The present
combination of balanced routing, recycling, and ERNTM separation targets
different parts of this tension. The combined model avoided gross collapse and
reduced duplication relative to earlier direct neural attempts, but the
evidence does not assign causal credit to each mechanism separately, and the
model does not reproduce the coherent long tail obtained by the mature
classical sampler.

\subsection{What ERNTM solved and what remains}
The model moved from the earlier K=1000 neural-assignment result---257 active
topics but diversity 0.3646---to fresh K=500 results of diversity 0.7354 and
231.5 mass-99 distinct-topic equivalents (0.7592 and 238.7 in the exact replay).
This is substantive structural progress, not just a relaxed threshold.
Nevertheless, NPMI and chemical coverage remain clearly below Tomotopy. The
appropriate claim is therefore
``working collapse-resistant fully neural discovery,'' not ``parity.''

DreaMS, a pretrained mass-spectrum representation model, is non-essential to
this contribution. The active evidence establishes that the current model
reconstructs its vocabulary and all features from raw training spectra; it
does not attempt to quantify the benefit or cost of DreaMS. Its exclusion
reduces dependencies and makes the claim narrower: discovered structure comes
from spectral co-occurrence, mass/type features, and the declared neural
objectives.

\subsection{Applied-metabolomics value}
Even without parity, one-pass inference can support high-throughput projection,
online screening, differentiable downstream retrieval, and rapid exploratory
analysis. The bundled K=500 model is also compact (approximately 6.5 megabytes
plus metadata) and directly yields both $\theta$ and $\beta$. The checkpoint
therefore provides a defensible research foundation for improving neural topic
quality and for tasks where amortized inference or differentiable integration
matters.

\section{Limitations}
This is a fixed single-seed checkpoint and does not estimate between-seed
uncertainty. Capacity and method families were compared on one deterministic
split; the test comparisons among validation-selected families are exploratory.
NPMI is an imperfect proxy for human or chemical interpretability
\cite{hoyle2022}. MAG coverage depends on the reference library, Spec2Vec, and
fingerprint procedure. Throughput is a cached CPU benchmark rather than an
end-to-end acquisition latency or a same-core comparison. The committed
headline evidence does not preserve neural training wall time, so no training
speed claim is made. The machine record identifies the run as Arm 64-bit
(arm64) but does not record a processor model. Finally, the ERNTM constraint is adapted to
the MS/MS router rather than claimed as a verbatim reproduction of the text
model.

\section{Conclusion}
A fully neural MS2LDA is possible in the limited but useful sense demonstrated
here: it can discover a substantial non-redundant motif inventory without a
classical teacher or VB, remain numerically stable, yield evaluable annotations
with measurable conditional structure overlap, and infer spectra in one fast
pass. It does not yet match Tomotopy's
coherence or chemical coverage. The recommended research direction is to
close those gaps while preserving collapse resistance and one-pass inference,
using the frozen K=500 ERNTM architecture as the starting point.

\section{Reproducibility}
The public acquisition script verifies Zenodo metadata, archive MD5/SHA-256,
ZIP integrity, extraction paths, links, required bytes, and required SHA-256 hashes.
The lean neural environment uses Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.6.0, NumPy 1.25.2,
SciPy 1.13.1, RDKit 2024.9.6, and Tomotopy 0.13.0. MAG is isolated because its
pinned stack differs: Python 3.12.12, Spec2Vec 0.9.1, FAISS-CPU 1.10.0,
MatchMS 0.29.0, NumPy 1.26.4, and RDKit 2024.9.6. The acquisition and run
commands are deliberately separate; \texttt{run} does not download data:
\begin{verbatim}
conda env create -f environment-neural-ms2lda.yml
conda env create -f environment-msnlib-mag.yml
conda run -n ms2lda-neural python \
  scripts/download_msnlib_validation_assets.py \
  --data-root /path/to/MSnLib-assets
conda run -n ms2lda-neural python -m \
  benchmarks.neural_assignment_ms2lda run \
  --data-root /path/to/MSnLib-assets --run /path/to/run
conda run -n ms2lda-neural python -m \
  benchmarks.neural_assignment_ms2lda status \
  --run /path/to/run
conda run -n ms2lda-neural python -m \
  benchmarks.neural_assignment_ms2lda verify \
  --data-root /path/to/MSnLib-assets --run /path/to/run
\end{verbatim}
After initialization, the resumable pipeline executes: prepare and freeze the
split and vocabulary; build four paired training views; train SGNS; construct
token features and prototypes; train and select the neural checkpoint;
evaluate neural test completion; train and evaluate Tomotopy; run neural and
Tomotopy chemistry in the isolated MAG environment; generate the report; and
verify manifests.

The pipeline records the resolved protocol, code hashes, environment, raw
input hashes, split manifest, selected checkpoint hash, and output manifests.
It resumes SGNS, neural training, and Tomotopy from verified checkpoints. The
committed model bundle contains the fresh validation-selected weights
(including token features), vocabulary, protocol, evaluation summaries,
chemistry summaries, portable provenance hashes, and a hash manifest. The
active weights are the fresh epoch-38 checkpoint. The historical epoch-36
weights are not retained in the active bundle; only their exact replay evidence
is committed. The machine-specific run directory and its large
$\theta$/$\beta$ arrays remain external to the repository.

\section{Unsuccessful approaches}
The concise experimental ledger is committed alongside this report. It records
random-start amortized collapse, warm-start limitations, hybrid boundaries,
the neural K=1000 activity/diversity trade-off, ECR capacity results, and the
reason K=500 ERNTM was selected. Implementation code for rejected arms remains
in the external all-refs safety bundle and preserved archive branches, not in
active main.

\section{Glossary for newcomers}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed,nosep,leftmargin=0pt,labelindent=0pt,labelsep=0.5em]
\item[MS2LDA / LDA] Mass-spectrometry latent Dirichlet allocation; LDA is the
classical document--topic--word admixture model adapted to spectra.
\item[MS/MS, MSnLib, MGF, and $m/z$] Tandem mass spectrometry; the public
multistage mass-spectral library benchmark used here; Mascot Generic Format;
and mass-to-charge ratio.
\item[Mass2Motif] A reusable distribution over fragment and neutral-loss words,
analogous to a topic in text.
\item[$K$, $\theta$, and $\beta$] Requested number of topics; a spectrum's
topic-mixture vector; and the topic--word probability matrix.
\item[VB, VAE, and KL] Variational Bayes; variational autoencoder; and
Kullback--Leibler divergence. None is used for neural spectrum inference here.
\item[SGNS and IDF] Skip-gram with negative sampling; inverse document
frequency, which upweights words occurring in fewer documents.
\item[MLP, JS, and AdamW] Multilayer perceptron; Jensen--Shannon divergence;
and the adaptive optimizer with decoupled weight decay used for model training.
\item[Sinkhorn balancing] Iterative row/column normalization used to construct
a balanced training target across topic prototypes; it is not a claim of
uniform chemical prevalence.
\item[ERNTM] Embedding-regularized neural topic model. This work adapts its
topic-embedding separation constraint, not the entire published text model.
\item[ECR / ECRTM] Embedding clustering regularization and its published topic
model; an explored alternative, not the selected model.
\item[NLL and NPMI] Negative log-likelihood and normalized pointwise mutual
information; predictive fit and top-word co-occurrence diagnostics,
respectively.
\item[OOV] Out of vocabulary: a held-out word absent from the training-only
vocabulary.
\item[SMILES and InChIKey] Text encodings of molecular structure and a hashed
International Chemical Identifier; the connectivity block ignores stereochemical
and other suffix information for leakage grouping.
\item[Bemis--Murcko scaffold] A molecule's ring-and-linker core, used to keep
structurally related cyclic compounds in one data partition.
\item[MAG, Spec2Vec, FAISS, and MACCS] Automated Mass2Motif Annotation
Guidance; a learned spectral-similarity embedding; a vector similarity-search
library; and a standard structural-key chemical fingerprint family.
\item[SOS] Structure overlap score: the fraction of consensus annotation bits
contained in a held-out molecule, averaged compound-first and then topic-first.
\item[DreaMS] A pretrained mass-spectrum representation model that is not used
by this checkpoint.
\item[CPU, CLI, JSON, MD5, and SHA-256] Central processing unit; command-line
interface; JavaScript Object Notation; Message Digest 5; and 256-bit Secure
Hash Algorithm. The last two identify and verify files rather than model
quality.
\end{description}
