Validation Checkpoint and Model Selection}

\begin{abstract}
Mass2Motif discovery requires more than good held-out likelihood. A useful model
must retain a broad, non-redundant topic inventory and assign short tandem mass
spectra strongly enough for chemical evaluation. Canonical and balanced Embedded
Topic Models (ETMs) retained reasonable likelihood but produced diffuse document
mixtures, while a sparse ETM using entmax alone starved most topics.

We therefore retained the published ETM generator and added two bounded inference
adaptations: top-2 contextual evidence from observed spectral words and published
alpha-entmax 1.5. The resulting model has only one additional learned scalar.
Across three unchanged training seeds on the locked seed-42, $K=1000$
MS$^{n}$Lib validation split, it produces 439--453 evaluable and 274--289 useful
motifs, compared with 408/265 for the private M1 reference and 206/138 for
Tomotopy. Median effective topics remain 3.70--3.71, 813--828 topics win at least
one validation spectrum, and no catastrophic duplicate component appears.

The model does not dominate every metric. Across the same runs it produces
787--803 optimized motifs, mean SOS 0.637558--0.647350 and completion NLL
9.539388--9.546012, versus 884, 0.658079 and 8.974140 for M1. Three conservative
M1-relative gates are therefore consistently false and candidate test remains
locked. The correct scientific conclusion is a strong near-pass and viable
paper-facing baseline: the discovery breadth, sparsity and inventory repairs are
initialization-stable on this split, while residual chemical-quality and
likelihood trade-offs also reproduce. A final weight-1 positive-NPMI add-on
improved its own train-graph objective but reduced planted beta recovery, so it
stopped at the first synthetic gate and was never run on real validation. The
last plausible zero-parameter simplification, direct top-2 token voting without
spectral context, also failed its first gate: beta/theta recovery fell by
0.0881/0.1034. This isolates the one learned context scalar as necessary in the
simplest demonstrated formulation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scientific objective}

MS2LDA represents tandem mass spectra as documents, fragment and neutral-loss
tokens as words, and recurrent co-fragmentation patterns as Mass2Motifs
\cite{vanderhooft2016}. At $K=1000$, a useful model must simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item predict held-out spectral evidence;
  \item retain many distinct topic-word distributions;
  \item assign each short spectrum to only a small number of motifs;
  \item produce enough confident spectrum-topic associations for MAG/SOS; and
  \item retain chemically useful motifs under leakage control.
\end{enumerate}

Likelihood alone can hide major failures. Topics may be starved, duplicated or
nearly uniform, and a spectrum may spread probability over dozens of topics even
when completion NLL is good. Earlier ETM experiments reproduced both failure
classes. This study asks whether a recognizable ETM base can be adapted to the
short MS/MS document regime without rebuilding the private M1 system.

\section{Evidence and metric contract}

The experiment uses the positive-mode MS$^{n}$Lib assets from Zenodo record
20179680 \cite{brungs2025,msnlibdata}. The locked scaffold/compound-disjoint split
contains 27,222 training and 3,889 validation spectra. Vocabulary and
48-dimensional SGNS features are constructed from training spectra only.
Physical peak groups are divided as units for document completion, preventing a
fragment and its corresponding neutral loss from leaking across observed and
withheld halves.

MAG retrieves leakage-filtered reference spectra and optimizes motif annotations.
The frozen validation membership threshold is $\theta_{dk}\geq0.5$. The primary
metrics are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{optimized motifs}: topics for which MAG produces at least one
  optimized annotation feature;
  \item \textbf{evaluable motifs}: optimized topics associated with at least one
  unique validation molecule;
  \item \textbf{SOS}: annotation-fingerprint containment in a molecule
  fingerprint, averaged over unique molecules and then over topics;
  \item \textbf{useful motifs}: evaluable topics with SOS $\geq0.6$; and
  \item \textbf{completion NLL}: negative log-likelihood per in-vocabulary
  withheld token under $\theta\beta$, where lower is better.
\end{itemize}

Candidate selection used training plus validation only. Candidate test theta,
completion, MAG, SOS and result files were not loaded, calculated or inspected.
The committed access audit records this boundary explicitly.

\section{Routing-informed sparse ETM}

\subsection{Published ETM generator}

Let $\rho_w\in\mathbb{R}^{48}$ be the fixed train-only SGNS coordinate of word
$w$ and $\alpha_k\in\mathbb{R}^{48}$ the learned ETM coordinate of topic $k$.
The decoder is the ordinary embedding-space topic model \cite{dieng2020}, with
separate fragment and neutral-loss normalizations so that each channel receives
half of every topic's probability mass:
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{kw}=\begin{cases}
  \tfrac12\softmax_{v\in V_F}(\alpha_k^\top\rho_v), & w\in V_F,\\
  \tfrac12\softmax_{v\in V_L}(\alpha_k^\top\rho_v), & w\in V_L.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The two-layer ETM encoder returns Gaussian parameters $\mu(x)$ and $\sigma(x)$
for normalized spectrum bag-of-words $x$. The variational family, standard-normal
analytic KL, raw pseudo-count multinomial reconstruction and optimizer remain
unchanged.

\subsection{Top-2 contextual posterior evidence}

For each non-zero word, the model combines its normalized SGNS vector with the
weighted leave-one-out mean of the other observed words. One learned scalar $c$
controls the context contribution:
\begin{equation}
  h_{dw}=\normalize\!\left(
  \normalize(\rho_w)+c\,\frac{\sum_vx_{dv}\rho_v-x_{dw}\normalize(\rho_w)}
  {\max(1-x_{dw},\epsilon)}\right).
\end{equation}

The contextual word is compared with the same normalized topic coordinates that
define $\beta$:
\begin{equation}
  s_{dwk}=\frac{h_{dw}^{\top}\normalize(\alpha_k)}{\tau_r},
  \qquad \tau_r=1.0.
\end{equation}
Only its two largest topic logits are retained (the top-2 route) and normalized
with a softmax. Their word-weighted sum yields one document evidence
distribution $r_d$.

The ordinary ETM posterior mean receives a bounded row-centred log offset:
\begin{align}
  \widetilde\mu(x_d)
    &=\mu(x_d)+\centerop\!\left[\log\left(r_d+\tfrac1K\right)\right],\\
  z_d&=\widetilde\mu(x_d)+\sigma(x_d)\odot\varepsilon,
       \qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\\
  \theta_d&=\entmax(z_d),\\
  p(w\mid d)&=\sum_k\theta_{dk}\beta_{kw}.
\end{align}

The $1/K$ pseudocount bounds topics absent from the sparse route. Row centring
makes uniform evidence an exact no-op. Alpha-entmax 1.5 supplies exact zeros
\cite{peters2019}. Routing temperature and strength are fixed at 1.0. The learned
context scalar finished at 0.1772, and total parameters increase from 19,278,000
for balanced ETM to 19,278,001.

There is no nonlinear M1 router, document gate, Sinkhorn target, NPMI loss,
prototype-separation loss, alternating optimizer or temperature schedule. The
paper-facing claim is therefore ETM plus one explainable short-spectrum posterior
adaptation and a published sparse simplex mapping.

\section{Historical alternatives and negative evidence}

The campaign tested one mechanism at a time and retained negative results:
\begin{itemize}
  \item canonical fixed-SGNS ETM had good completion but only 130 evaluable and
  79 useful motifs, with 43.79 median effective topics;
  \item fragment/loss balancing raised optimized coverage from 609 to 911 but
  did not repair evaluable/useful breadth or SOS;
  \item post-hoc temperature sharpening changed mixture scale but could not
  jointly preserve breadth, SOS and NLL;
  \item pooled projected models contained a 614-topic near-exact beta component;
  \item entmax without routing produced exact sparsity by starving the inventory;
  \item the maintained ECRTM Sinkhorn solver failed its convergence contract at
  real $K=1000$ and vocabulary 21,233, so no partial model was scored; and
  \item a reference short-document NSTM failed the paired high-K synthetic screen
  and was not promoted to MSnLib.
\end{itemize}

These results justify the selected posterior adaptation without claiming that
every future topic model must fail. They also show why another likelihood-only
comparison would be insufficient.

\section{Runtime and system load}

The original frozen model trained for 120 epochs in 876.8 seconds (14.6 minutes)
on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070. The two added seeds trained in 795.4 and 798.5
seconds (about 13.3 minutes each). Including final inference and evidence
hashing, each run completed in roughly 15--16 minutes. Observed GPU load ranged
from moderate to high; one repeat snapshot recorded 40\% utilization,
54~$^{\circ}$C, 110 W of a 250 W limit and 2.6/12.2 GB device memory in use.

Peak PyTorch allocated/reserved CUDA memory was 0.821/1.053 GB. Process high-water
memory was 2.879 GB, with at least 12.43 GB system memory remaining. Deterministic
full-validation inference processed approximately 23,426 spectra per second. The
load was sustained but comfortably below GPU memory, thermal, power and system
memory limits.

\section{Limitations and robustness}

The three real runs establish descriptive initialization stability on one
positive-mode data split: the sample is small and intentionally changes neither
data membership nor hyperparameters. It does not establish generalization to
other splits, instruments, ionization modes, biological samples, datasets or
topic counts.

The positive-NPMI stopping result is one synthetic seed because its predeclared
first-stage gate failed. It supports rejection of this fixed add-on, not a broad
claim that all possible coherence objectives are ineffective.

The zero-parameter routing result is likewise one synthetic seed because four
primary non-inferiority conditions failed at the first stage. It supports
retaining the contextual scalar; it is not a claim that no future encoder could
use a different parameter-free context representation.

MAG/SOS is a library-dependent chemical proxy. Higher evaluable breadth can
lower an unweighted mean by admitting additional borderline topics, which is why
counts, mean, median and SOS bands are reported together. Expert interpretation
and independent-dataset replication remain necessary.

M1 is retained as a locked private comparator and donor-ablation source, not as
the proposed publication architecture. Tomotopy remains the independent
production comparator. No claim is made that Routing ETM should replace the
production backend before independent and untouched-test review.

\section{Recommended next evidence}

The baseline and stability checkpoints should be preserved before further model
changes. The next steps are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item verify both committed source/evidence packages and retained local
  artifacts;
  \item stop repeating identical same-split seeds; future robustness work should
  vary the split or dataset;
  \item do not tune the failed NPMI coefficient or add another private-donor
  component;
  \item retain the one-scalar leave-one-out context rather than replacing it
  with the failed token-only route;
  \item prefer a different split or external library over further architecture
  search on the same validation evidence; and
  \item unlock candidate test only after an independent review freezes the
  method, checkpoint and success interpretation.
\end{enumerate}

Do not add M1's document gate, Sinkhorn balancing, prototype separation,
alternating optimizer or temperature schedule. Additional complexity now risks
making an already strong and explainable result harder to publish.

\section{Conclusion}

Routing-informed sparse ETM repairs the central failure of neural topic models
on short MS/MS documents: it achieves sparse per-spectrum mixtures without
collapsing or starving the global topic inventory. It exceeds both M1 and
Tomotopy on evaluable and useful validation motif counts, while retaining a
recognizable ETM generator and adding only one learned scalar.

The result is not uniform dominance. M1 retains better optimized coverage, mean
SOS and completion NLL; Tomotopy has higher SOS over fewer evaluable motifs. The
predeclared all-gates Boolean remains false and candidate test remains locked.
Nevertheless, the scientific outcome is positive: Routing ETM is the strongest
paper-facing discovery baseline produced by this campaign, and its central
advantage is stable across the three tested initializations. Future work should
preserve explainability and test new sources of generalization. The failed NPMI
triage reinforces that the simplest supported formulation is the existing
Routing ETM, not Routing ETM plus additional donor mechanisms. The failed
zero-parameter ablation closes the opposite direction as well: removing
leave-one-out context loses too much recovery and inventory for the saving of a
single scalar.
